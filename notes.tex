\clearpage\section{Fair Market Value}

To figure out how much room there is for negotiation, research the car’s value ahead of time. This gives you a baseline to work from.
Tools to use:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Kelley Blue Book (KBB): Enter the car’s make, model, year, mileage, and condition to get an estimate of its market value.
    \item Edmunds: Check the True Market Value (TMV) of the car, which shows the average price paid for that specific model in your area.
    \item Autotrader: Look at listings for similar cars and see what others are asking for.
    \item CarGurus: This site tells you if a listing is a “good deal” or overpriced, based on the market.
\end{enumerate}

By looking up the FMV, you’ll know what others are paying for similar cars in your area. If the dealer’s price is higher than the market value, you’ve got room to negotiate down.

\subsection{Invoice Price}
Figure out the invoice-price (price paid by the showroom to the manufacturer)
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Edmunds and TrueCar: Both of these sites can give you the invoice price of the car.
    \item Consumer Reports: They offer data on pricing and what dealers are actually paying for vehicles.
\end{enumerate}

Try to get the price of the vehicle to be a few hundreds of dollars above the invoice price.

Make sure you're negotiating on the out-the-door price.

\subsection{Additional}
Ask if they wanna add more perks for that price
\begin{enumerate}
    \item free maintenance
    \item extended warranty
\end{enumerate}
